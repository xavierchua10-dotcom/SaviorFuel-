Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 2026

A plain-language summary from the app's independent developer, not a law firm. It is written to be readable rather than dense legal text.

1. The short version

SaviorFuel has no accounts, no sign-up and no cloud sync. What you log stays on your device. There are no ads and no analytics or tracking in the app, and your data is never sold.

2. What is stored on your device

Everything is saved in your browser's local storage on your own phone or computer:

  • The meals you log, with their nutrition numbers and the day and time
  • Your daily goals, plus the profile you enter to calculate them (sex, age, height, bodyweight, activity level and goal)
  • Water you log, your saved foods and your own "My Foods"
  • Your app settings (theme, units and similar)

We never receive this data. Clearing your browser data or using “Erase all data” in Settings removes it. Because it only lives on your device, it can be lost if you clear site data or lose the device, so use Export backup in Settings now and then.

3. What leaves your device

To look up foods, the app sends what you typed (or the barcode you scanned) to public food databases. These requests don't include your meals, goals or profile:

ServiceWhat it receives
Open Food FactsYour food search text or a scanned barcode, to find packaged foods and product photos
USDA FoodData CentralYour food search text, to find generic foods
Kalori (techapi.my)Your food search text, to find Malaysian foods
SaviorFuel AI server (Cloudflare) and Google GeminiA meal photo, only when you use Snap a photo and have agreed to it (see section 4)
Public CDNs (Google Fonts, cdnjs, jsDelivr)The app loads its fonts and a few code libraries (including the 3D viewer and the photo-recognition model) from these services. Like any website, they can see your IP address. They don't receive your food data.

Each of these services has its own privacy practices.

4. Camera and photos

The camera opens only when you choose to scan a barcode or snap a photo of a meal, and only while the scanner screen is open. Barcodes are read on your device.

AI photo scan. When you snap a photo of a meal, the app asks for your consent the first time. If you agree, the photo is sent to a small server run by the developer (on Cloudflare), which passes it to Google’s Gemini AI to estimate what is on the plate. The photo is used only to produce that estimate: SaviorFuel does not save it and does not attach your name or account to it. To enforce a daily scan limit, the server keeps a count of scans per device address (a scrambled, one-way version of the IP address, which expires after two days). Google handles the photo under its own terms; on Google’s free tier, content sent to it may be used to improve its products, so avoid photos that show people, documents or anything private. If you decline, or the AI is unavailable, the app falls back to a basic guess made on your device and nothing is uploaded.

5. Your control

  • Export a full JSON backup of your data from Settings
  • Import a backup to restore it
  • Erase everything from Settings → Danger zone

6. Children

SaviorFuel isn't aimed at children under 13, and it does not knowingly collect any personal information from anyone.
